## 3. Make the business model explain why someone would pay

Headline:

**“Experience the value. Return to it. Choose to keep it.”**

Use a simple price strip:

**14 days free → $7.50/month for two months → $15/month thereafter.**

Keep “Proposed pricing experiment” visible. There is no permanent free tier in this plan. Do not introduce a different price, unlimited AI usage, or an unverified automatic billing flow.

Pair the pricing with three concrete reasons to return:

- A useful app already fits an interest or recurring task.
- Personal records make the next visit more valuable.
- The workspace can adapt when the person's needs change.

The intended trial experience is one useful app, a real action, and a later-day return. Explain the upgrade as choosing continued usefulness. Use language such as “become part of their routine” rather than “get users hooked.” Do not propose manipulative reminders, hidden charges, or making records inaccessible to force payment. Normal subscription access rules must be stated clearly when billing is actually implemented.

Put a compact measurement strip under the offer:

**Useful return → actual paid conversion → renewal → cost to serve.**

This is a business funnel, not a replacement for the five-stage product story. Keep its visual treatment subordinate.

Suggested speaker track, approximately 22 seconds:

> Every new user gets 14 days to make something useful and come back to it. Then we test seven-fifty a month for two months, followed by fifteen. The reason to pay is a collection that keeps fitting your life. We'll measure actual conversion, renewal, and the cost of serving both free-trial and paying users.

Keep these economic questions in speaker notes or the appendix:

- Trial conversion: actual first payments divided by trial starters whose trial window has finished. Report incomplete cohorts separately.
- Retention: meaningful weekly actions, with access and payment status visible. Day-14 expiry affects the interpretation of week-four usage.
- Full-price evidence: distinguish the first $15 charge from the next $15 renewal. An eight-week pilot does not establish full-price renewal under this pricing ladder.
- Serving cost: model calls, retries, hosting/storage, and support. Free-trial users incur costs even when they never subscribe.
- Acquisition cost: track founder time and cash by channel; do not claim free acquisition merely because the founder has relationships.
- Margin: compare net collected revenue with actual serving costs before claiming profitability or a gross-margin percentage. Do not treat a token-only estimate as complete cost.

The relevant claim is “We have designed a measurable business experiment,” not “We already know people will pay.”
